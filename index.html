The Problem

The 72-Hour Window.

When a structure collapses, responders have a 72-hour window to rescue survivors. Agencies in Bangladesh and South Asia rely on manual searching — a slow, dangerous process.

Current tactical UGVs are prohibitively expensive ($70K–$200K) and rely on heavy 3D sensors that choke constrained wireless networks in disaster zones.

The Four Barriers

  • 01Human responders cannot safely enter unstable structures.
  • 02Existing robotic solutions are too expensive for developing nations.
  • 03Dense 3D mapping data (15–21 Mbps) fails over ad-hoc networks.
  • 04Visual cameras fail in zero-visibility, smoke-filled environments.
Our Solution

Computational Offloading
& Edge AI.

We've engineered and simulation-validated an autonomous rover architecture that maps in 3D using only lightweight 2D data, while detecting survivors via thermally-trained AI.

Push-Broom 3D Mapping

The rover sends lightweight 2D LiDAR slices (85ms delay). A base-station algorithm reconstructs the full 3D map from odometry — no heavy sensor streams.

YOLOv11m Thermal AI

Custom-trained on OpenThermalPose with CLAHE augmentation, achieving 89.2% mAP@50 to detect partially occluded survivors in complete darkness.

Under $2,000 Hardware

Dual-ESP microcontroller architecture with D-Robotics RDK X5 compute module. A fraction of the cost of any foreign alternative.
