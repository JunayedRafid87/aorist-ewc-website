The Problem

The 72-Hour Window.

When a structure collapses, responders have a 72-hour window to rescue survivors. Today, agencies in Bangladesh and South Asia rely on manual searching — a slow, dangerous process.


Current tactical UGVs are prohibitively expensive ($70K–$200K) and rely on heavy 3D sensors that choke constrained wireless networks in disaster zones.

The Limitations We Face

  • Human responders cannot safely enter unstable structures.
  • Existing robotic solutions are too expensive for developing nations.
  • Dense 3D mapping data (15–21 Mbps) fails over congested ad-hoc networks.
  • Visual cameras fail in zero-visibility, smoke-filled environments.
Our Solution

Computational Offloading & Edge AI.

We've engineered and simulation-validated an autonomous rover architecture that maps in 3D using only lightweight 2D data, while detecting survivors via thermally-trained AI.

Push-Broom 3D Mapping

The rover sends lightweight 2D LiDAR slices (85ms delay). A base-station algorithm reconstructs the full 3D map from odometry.

YOLOv11m Thermal AI

Custom-trained on OpenThermalPose with CLAHE augmentation, achieving 89.2% mAP@50 to detect partially occluded survivors.

Under $2,000 Hardware

Designed with a dual-ESP microcontroller architecture and D-Robotics RDK X5 compute module. Targeting a fraction of the cost of foreign alternatives.
